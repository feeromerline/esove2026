Mosquito population mixing shapes Rift Valley fever transmission across a wildlife–livestock interface

Merline Feero, Tino Pori, Peter Thompson, Bree Beechler, Anna Jolles, Morgan Bar, Erin Gorsich

About this poster

Rift Valley fever circulates in African buffalo in Kruger National Park between epidemics, while cattle just outside the park fence show little sign of infection. We combined host serology, mosquito blood-meal analysis and a mechanistic model to ask how mosquitoes feeding across the park boundary link the two.

At the Kruger interface, RVF can circulate in buffalo independently of cattle. Cattle exposure depends on mosquitoes feeding across the park boundary and, at the field-measured mixing rates, stays below detection. Modelling results are preliminary.
